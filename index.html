Stage 1

What happened

Describe the event in neutral words, by role and not by person. The review looks for how the system made the event possible, not for someone to blame.

Roles involved

Each role becomes a swimlane. Use roles such as "Night nurse" or "On-call resident", never names. Steps are added in stage 2 and drawn in each role's lane in stage 3. Open a role to see, edit or add its steps. Click a role's name to rename it.

    Stage 2

    Build the timeline, one step at a time

    For each step, answer the questions as the person in that role would have seen it at the time. The question about what they believed is the one that surfaces hidden assumptions. Every step appears in its role's lane in stage 3, with its handoffs and dependencies drawn as arrows.

        Stage 4

        Shared responsibilities

        List the tasks that more than one role touches. Then ask each role: did you believe this was yours, or another role's? A task nobody believed was theirs is an unclaimed step. Unclaimed steps often sit at the centre of an event and rarely show up in a single person's account.

        Stage 5

        Causes

        Group contributing factors in a cause and effect diagram. For each factor, keep asking "Why?" and add the answer as a sub-cause, until a cause is specific enough to test a change against. Then write causal statements that link a cause to the event. The review team decides the causes; the tool only suggests factors from the timeline and checks the wording of each statement.

            Stage 6

            Actions

            Each causal statement should lead to at least one action. Actions that change the system, such as forcing functions or simpler processes, tend to hold up better than actions that rely on people remembering, such as training or a new policy. Give each action a measure and the point when its test starts; stage 7 then shows whether it made a difference.

              Stage 7

              Did the actions work?

              Each action from stage 6 is a test of change. Choose a measure to see its run chart. The point where each linked action's test started is marked on the chart, so you can see whether the change made a difference. With 10 or more points the chart draws the median.

              Actions tested on this measure

              Shift and trend signals use common run chart rules: six or more points in a row on one side of the median is a shift; five or more points in a row going the same direction is a trend. These rules are not part of the IHI run chart instructions this tool follows, and are signals to discuss, not proof.

              About this tool

              This is an early prototype built on the ICA Project Cloud model: each role is a workstream with its own lane, a shared responsibility is a constraint several workstreams touch, and handoffs are exchanges across lane boundaries. A review shows where independently organized work met and failed to connect.

              The methods follow quality improvement tools described by the Institute for Healthcare Improvement (cause and effect diagram, flowchart and run chart, from the QI Essentials Toolkit) and the RCA² approach to root cause analysis, including its rules for causal statements and its action hierarchy. All wording here is original. This tool is not affiliated with or endorsed by IHI. The original materials are at ihi.org.

              The tool records what people tell it. It does not decide root causes, rate anyone's performance, or determine fault.
